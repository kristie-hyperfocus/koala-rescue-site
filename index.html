What to do if you see a koala

1
phone_in_talk

Phone a rescuer — calls only, no texts

If you have any concerns about the koala's health, safety or location, please call a rescuer for advice or help as soon as possible, and preferably stay with the koala. Do not text or Facebook message, as we might miss your message.

Stay with the koala and observe

Please remain nearby but keep pets and people back.

When calling, you may be asked:

  • Exact location: address, GPS or landmark. Tag the tree with tape if possible.
  • Cause for concern?
  • Situation: on the ground or up a tree?
  • Tree height?
  • Koala height in the tree?
  • Is the koala awake — looking around, moving, or curled up asleep?
  • Crowds or dogs nearby?
  • Environment: quiet street, busy road, bushland, private property?
3
support_agent

A rescuer will assess and advise

Based on what you tell us, the rescuer will be able to determine the right course of action. Your observations guide our response: every detail matters.

Signs that a koala may need help

Koalas should be in trees. If you notice any of the following, please call us immediately.

Phone us promptly on 0424 807 470, if you notice:

  • water_drop
    Bottom is wet, dirty or brown
  • visibility
    Eyes are red, inflamed or crusty
  • accessibility_new
    Body is thin: spine or hip bones are visible
  • arrow_circle_down
    Sitting low in a tree or on the ground
  • calendar_month
    In the same tree for 3–4 days or more
  • healing
    Injuries visible: cuts, blood, lost fur, or not using all 4 limbs
  • child_care
    Orphan joey: smaller than a pineapple and not near mum
  • directions_car
    Near roads or dogs (even if it looks fine)
  • crisis_alert
    At risk: near a road, no habitat nearby, or looks lost
  • help
    You aren't sure: when in doubt, call us
shield Safety first: Don't touch the koala, even if it seems friendly. Injured animals can bite and scratch. Keep your distance and please PHONE us promptly.

Who we are

Real people, real work

We're a diverse group of volunteers from Brisbane's southern suburbs, united by one mission: to protect koalas. Many of us have full-time jobs; others are retirees. Every volunteer is important to the ongoing viability of Koala Rescue Brisbane South.

Founded by John Knights, who has over 15 years of wildlife rescue experience, Koala Rescue Brisbane South combines professional expertise with genuine community care.

We don't just rescue. We relocate koalas within their home ranges when necessary and we release them after wildlife veterinary care. Importantly, we keep learning about koalas, their behaviours, their needs and their habitat from every single rescue.
